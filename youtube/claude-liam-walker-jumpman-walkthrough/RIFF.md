# RIFF — what Liam says over the action, and why

Riffs are written after inspecting each take, not from the design document.
Each one names something visible and the trade-off behind it.

**B02 — the premise.** The riff is about restraint: the game states the rule in
six words on a sign and never explains it again. The trade-off is that a player
who does not read signs starts confused — and the first playtest proved that
risk was real.

**B03 — falling ground.** "Keep moving" is the one sign in the chapter that is
telling the truth. Worth naming, because four later signs are not.

**B04 — the feather.** The interesting part is the economy, not the pickup. One
charge that recharges is a different game from three charges that do not: it
makes checking free, and checking is the verb the chapter is about.

**B05 — the flip.** Two readouts change at once — the trail colour and the whole
palette. The trade-off is redundancy: two cues for one state is wasteful unless
the state is the entire point, which here it is.

**B06 — the Void Gap.** The riff is that nothing was added to the physics. The
floor is an ordinary collider. Only the drawing withholds it.

**B07 — the Betrayal.** The honest riff: this punishes the player for learning
the lesson the previous screen taught. The defence is the sign, the visible
ceiling route, and a free check. Whether that defence holds is a question for a
player, and the verdict says so.

**B08 — signs that lie.** Four claims, three false. By this point the game has
trained the player to verify, which is the only reason lying is fair.

**B10 — failure.** The retry is the starter's, untouched. Everything resets with
the attempt: charges, gravity, collapsed ledges, collected logs.

**B11 — the tower.** The finish is above the player inside a shaft. There is a
test that asserts a normal jump cannot reach it, so the mechanic is load-bearing
rather than decorative.
